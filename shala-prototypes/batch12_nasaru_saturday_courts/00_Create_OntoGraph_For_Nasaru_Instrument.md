Every formal concept in the lattice is a set of objects sharing a set of attributes, and that extent *is* a hyperedge: one edge binding many particles at once, not a chain of pairwise links. So OntoGraph isn't bolting a hypergraph onto Nasaru; it's naming the structure that Metamodel + FCA was always going to emit.

The three stages map onto sealed machinery like this. The ontological pass belongs to EnkiDDB — the W5H2 clause words and Sowa conceptual relations (GL-DDB-001) give you the vocabulary for what each scanned particle *is* and how it relates, all as EAV Mandatory Attributes. OntoGraph's real contribution is the fusion: the FCA context is built with objects = particles (addressed by KAKI v4.0) and attributes = the union of ontological EAV facets and topological classes. Run the closure, and the concepts that survive are your nodes; the concept lattice with its extent-hyperedges is the Unified Pattern. That pattern is then sealed as a template particle under GL-TPL-001 with its own Hepta Space coordinates, which means Nasaru visualizes it the same way it visualizes any other tribe — no special-case renderer.

## Q:
Do you mean "all as mandatory EAV-style attributes" or "all as optional EAV-style attributes"? After all, mandatory attributes are those strictly tied to Kakiv4.0 and cannot be part of it—given its immutability—whereas any optional attributes could encompass all (or any) organizational attributes that align with DMBOK semantics and terminology; is that correct?
## Ans:
Both — split by role, and the split is the same one your existing laws already draw.

Your framing is right on the essential point: nothing that describes a particle's meaning, quality, or organization ever enters the sixteen KAKI bytes. Where I'd sharpen it is the word "tied." Mandatory EAV attributes aren't tied to KAKI in the sense of being derived from its bytes; they are the sealed minimum every particle must carry, keyed by its KAKI. Their *set* is immutable — sealed by law — but their *values* move, which is the whole point of Triple-O: the state trichotomy, ColourID via PASHIRU, freshness, and the domain marker sit there precisely because they change while the KAKI does not. So the layering is: KAKI = immutable address; Mandatory EAV = sealed schema, mutable values, guaranteed present; Optional EAV = open schema, discovered or organizational.

For OntoGraph that gives a clean rule. Into Mandatory go only the things already legislated there: the W5H2 clause words and their Sowa relations (GL-DDB-001), the GOLDEN/FUZZY/DEAD state class, ColourID, freshness, domain. Everything OntoGraph *discovers* — concept labels, lattice rank, hyperedge membership, Betti signature per concept — and everything DMBOK-flavored that an organization brings — business glossary term, data steward, data domain, lineage, classification, retention, quality dimension per DAMA — goes into Optional. That's also what makes the FCA context well-formed: the Mandatory set gives you the invariant columns every object is guaranteed to have, so the lattice always has a stable spine, while the Optional set is where the interesting closures appear.

One consequence: OntoGraph mints Optional attributes and templates, never Mandatory ones. If a discovered concept turns out to be universal across all seven EnkiDB types and you want it promoted to Mandatory, that's a law change through Gate G4 with Z3 proof, not a runtime decision.
